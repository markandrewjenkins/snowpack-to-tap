Method

Snow index. The ratio of summed snow water equivalent to summed 1991–2020 medians for the same day, across SNOTEL sites with records back to the mid-1980s. This mirrors the NRCS basin index.

Inflow forecast. Log April–July unregulated inflow to Lake Powell is regressed on that day's snow index, the water-year precipitation index and the previous autumn's baseflow, with one model per forecast date. It's validated leave-one-year-out, and the prediction intervals come from the cross-validated errors.

Lake projections. Each year since 2000, I take the lake's storage change from today's calendar date over the next 12–15 months, add it to today's storage, and convert to elevation with the lake's recent storage–elevation curve.

California. CDEC daily storage for the 12 largest reservoirs, compared with their 1991–2020 average within ±3 days of the date.

Honest limitations

  • Operations matter. Releases from Powell and Mead follow negotiated rules that changed in 2007 and 2019 and are being renegotiated for 2027 onward, so analog years replay old rules on today's water.
  • The inflow model has about 40 seasons to learn from. A snowpack outside that range (below about 50% or above about 160%) is an extrapolation.
  • Spring weather (dust on snow, a hot dry May) can move inflow by a million acre-feet after April 1. That's why the error never drops to zero.
  • SNOTEL sites sit at mid-to-high elevations. The Sierra index here is not DWR's official statewide index.
